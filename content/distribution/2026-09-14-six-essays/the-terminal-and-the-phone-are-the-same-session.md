## X/Twitter thread

1/ The wrongest assumption in my tooling: an agent session lives in a terminal window. It doesn't. The session is the state; every device is a viewport. New essay on what changed when I finally believed that.

2/ Once you say "the session is the state, the device is a viewport," three decisions stop being decisions: sessions live on machines that stay on; output is designed for the smallest viewport (the phone); and the interrupt becomes the interface — what mechanically requires the human?

3/ Designing for voice on a phone forced the best part: compressing a session into what changed, what's blocked, what needs me. That compression turned out to be what a tired founder needs at a desk at midnight. The smallest screen made the biggest screen better.

4/ The night it paid for itself: my orchestrator was burning money idle and no agent flagged it — I traced it by hand. But the trace took minutes *because* sessions were enumerable state on a server, not processes scattered across three machines' terminals.

5/ Honest label: HeyCLI is a working prototype, not a product — it says so on my /hire page. But the abstraction transferred everywhere: decide where the session's state lives, and mobility stops being a feature request and becomes a property you already had.

6/ The terminal was a fine viewport. It was a terrible place for the session to live. Full essay: https://abdur.ai/writing/the-terminal-and-the-phone-are-the-same-session
