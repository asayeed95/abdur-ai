## X/Twitter thread

1/ A solo founder has no editor. Add agent ghostwriters and you've built a machine for producing plausible claims. The question isn't whether it publishes something false — it's what stops it. New essay: evidence before claims, the rule with a build gate behind it.

2/ Every public claim on my site is typed: reported (this happened — here is the receipt), designed (this is what I'd build — nothing claimed to exist), argued (this is what I think — no artifact behind it). An untyped claim fails the build.

3/ Two hard lines underneath: no past-tense verb over an event that did not happen. No number attached to something that was not measured. Neither is a style preference — both are answers to failures I personally logged.

4/ The failure mode isn't lying. It's drift: "we aim for fast recall" → "recall is fast" → "recall is fast, verified" across drafts. You can't fix drift with vigilance, because vigilance is exactly what degrades at deadline. You make the boundary structural.

5/ The receipts habit changes the writing itself. "Industry-leading" has no receipt. "Blazing fast" has no receipt. What survives is narrower and stronger: this migration, at this SHA, repairs this table.

6/ I keep a ledger of every claim that shipped ahead of its evidence. Reading it back is the whole argument: honest people with no gate make false claims at the rate enthusiasm exceeds verification. The gate isn't an accusation — it's a prosthesis. Full essay: https://abdur.ai/writing/evidence-before-claims
